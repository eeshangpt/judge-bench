## A. Cascade by selective prediction
Hypothesis A: MiniCheck-FT5 can settle a useful share of items alone, at Cohen's kappa >= 0.9x the Sonnet benchmark on those items, and the rest can go to a causal small LM.
Method: confidence of MiniCheck c = |p_yes - 0.5|. For threshold tau, ACCEPTED = {c >= tau} (MiniCheck's own prediction is kept), REJECTED = {c < tau} (forwarded to Qwen3-4B, or Phi-4-mini, as two separate cascades). Fixed cut 0.5, no calib-tuned threshold in the primary analysis.
Metrics (standard):
- Coverage = |accepted| / n. Selective risk = errors among accepted / |accepted| (error = pred != label). Risk-coverage curve = selective risk vs coverage over all tau; AURC = area under it, ties in c broken by treating tied items as one step (Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017, "Selective Classification for Deep Neural Networks", NeurIPS). Raw AURC is not comparable across sets with different base error, so we also report E-AURC = AURC - AURC of the optimal ordering (Geifman, Uziel & El-Yaniv 2019, "Bias-Reduced Uncertainty Estimation for Deep Neural Classifiers", ICLR). AURC and E-AURC are descriptive, not part of any decision rule.
- Cohen's kappa (Cohen 1960): kappa = (po - pe) / (1 - pe), po = observed agreement, pe = chance agreement from the marginals.
- Primary quantity (A1): selective kappa = kappa of MiniCheck on the accepted set. Target = 0.9 x Sonnet kappa = 0.541 (RAGTruth), 0.493 (WiCE). A1 = the largest coverage with selective kappa >= target and |accepted| >= 30 (floor so that tiny subsets cannot fake a result). Co-reported next to every selective kappa: accepted-set prevalence, selective accuracy and selective risk, because kappa depends on prevalence and the Sonnet constant comes from a different sample, so the kappa ratio is indicative (open decision for the owner: keep kappa as the gate, as specified, or gate on selective risk).
- Threshold selection is out-of-sample: tau is chosen by 5-fold cross-fitting (seed 7, stratified by label). For each fold, tau* = the tau with the largest coverage meeting the A1 conditions on the other 4 folds; it is applied to the held-out fold. A1 coverage and selective kappa are computed on the pooled held-out accepts. The in-sample sweep (tau chosen on all n) is reported only as an upper-bound sensitivity.
- Secondary (A2, success side): cascade kappa on all n items (pooled out-of-fold) when rejected items take the LM's prediction. PRIMARY LM = Qwen3-4B (best job-03 RAGTruth kappa); Phi-4-mini is exploratory. Success needs A2 >= target. Also report LM calls saved = coverage, and cost = (1 - coverage) x LM p50 latency.
- 95% CI: percentile bootstrap over items, 1000 resamples, seed 7 (same as job 03), repeating the full cross-fit inside each resample, for A1 coverage, selective kappa, A2.
Decision rules (pending owner OK):
- KILL A if out-of-fold A1 coverage < 20% on both RAGTruth and WiCE (point estimate).
- SCALE A if out-of-fold A1 coverage >= 20% on both sets AND A2 (Qwen3-4B) >= target on both sets, with the bootstrap lower bound of A1 coverage > 0 on both. Single primary LM, so no multiplicity across LMs. Still reported as "to be confirmed on fresh items".
- Otherwise ITERATE.
Exploratory (not decision-bearing): Phi-4-mini cascade; calib-tuned cut instead of 0.5; confidence from the LMs instead of MiniCheck; a stacked (MiniCheck x LM) confidence; the other three sets (XSum, CNN, ExpertQA); A on the natural-mix 900 items of B (no Sonnet benchmark there, so only risk-coverage and AURC). Limitation stated: the A sets are class-balanced by construction (RAIM-matched), which is what makes the Sonnet constant comparable but is not the natural mix.

## B. Rank systems, not items
Hypothesis B: a small judge, applied to a generator's outputs, orders the six RAGTruth generators the way the human labels do.
Method: per generator g and judge j, judged unfaithful rate U(j,g) = share of the generator's 150 items with pred = 0 at the fixed 0.5 cut. Human rate H(g) as above. Ranking quality = Kendall's tau-b between H and U(j,.) over the 6 generators (15 pairs). tau = (concordant - discordant) / (n(n-1)/2); tau-b corrects for ties (Kendall 1938, 1945), needed because human or judged rates can tie.
CI: stratified bootstrap over items within each generator (1000 resamples, seed 7), recomputing BOTH H and U on every resample (the human rates are themselves estimated from 150 items per generator, so they are resampled, not fixed). Resamples where tau-b is undefined (all ties) count as tau-b = 0 (conservative) and their number is reported.
Primary judge for B = Qwen3-4B (best job-03 RAGTruth kappa); MiniCheck and Phi-4-mini are exploratory, so no multiplicity across judges. Tiers (named from the human rates): High = llama-2-7b; Mid = llama-2-13b, llama-2-70b, mistral-7B; Low = gpt-3.5, gpt-4. Tier order is correct iff the pooled judged unfaithful rate satisfies U(High) > U(Mid) > U(Low), pooling the generators of a tier.
Decision rules (pending owner OK): KILL B if tau-b < 0.5 or the 95% CI includes 0 for the primary judge. SCALE B if tau-b >= 0.5 with CI excluding 0 for the primary judge AND tier order is correct. Else ITERATE.
Power caveat stated up front: with n=6 the tau values are multiples of 2/15 and the CI is wide; differences of 1 to 6 items among the Mid generators are noise. A pass means the three tiers are recovered, not the fine order inside Mid. Tier-level concordance (the tier-order check above) is the more robust of the two criteria.
Secondary (exploratory): MiniCheck and Phi-4-mini tau-b; item-level Cohen's kappa and bacc of each judge on the 900 (natural, imbalanced mix; kappa not comparable to the balanced job-03 numbers); mean(1 - p_yes) as an alternative generator score; Spearman rho as a cross-check; the train split (839 per generator) as a second, unconditional ranking, only if the owner approves ~15 more GPU hours. No sample-expansion rule depends on the test result.
