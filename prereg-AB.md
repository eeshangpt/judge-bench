## A. Cascade by selective prediction
Hypothesis A: MiniCheck-FT5 can settle a useful share of items alone, at Cohen's kappa >= 0.9x the Sonnet benchmark on those items, and the rest can go to a causal small LM.
Method: confidence of MiniCheck c = |p_yes - 0.5|. For threshold tau, ACCEPTED = {c >= tau} (MiniCheck's own prediction is kept), REJECTED = {c < tau} (forwarded to Qwen3-4B, or Phi-4-mini, as two separate cascades). Fixed cut 0.5, no calib-tuned threshold in the primary analysis.
Metrics (standard):
- Coverage = |accepted| / n. Selective risk = errors among accepted / |accepted| (error = pred != label). Risk-coverage curve = selective risk vs coverage over all tau; AURC = area under it, ties in c broken by treating tied items as one step (Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017, "Selective Classification for Deep Neural Networks", NeurIPS). Raw AURC is not comparable across sets with different base error, so we also report E-AURC = AURC - AURC of the optimal ordering (Geifman, Uziel & El-Yaniv 2019, "Bias-Reduced Uncertainty Estimation for Deep Neural Classifiers", ICLR). AURC and E-AURC are descriptive, not part of any decision rule.
- Cohen's kappa (Cohen 1960): kappa = (po - pe) / (1 - pe), po = observed agreement, pe = chance agreement from the marginals.
- Primary quantity (A1, the gate, owner decision 'kappa for now'): selective kappa = kappa of MiniCheck on the accepted set. Target = 0.9 x Sonnet kappa = 0.541 (RAGTruth), 0.493 (WiCE). A1 = the largest coverage with selective kappa >= target and |accepted| >= 30 (floor so that tiny subsets cannot fake a result). Selective risk is co-reported, NOT a gate: cascade error (accepted use MiniCheck, rejected use Qwen3-4B) minus Qwen3-4B-alone error on all items, with CI. For every operating point we also report the 2x2 confusion table of the accepted subset, its label prevalence (share faithful), the full-set prevalence, and balanced accuracy as a descriptive row (Brodersen et al. 2010, "The balanced accuracy and its posterior distribution", ICPR; bacc = (TPR + TNR)/2), so the prevalence effect on kappa is visible. The Sonnet constant comes from a different sample, so the kappa ratio is indicative.
- Threshold selection is out-of-sample: tau is chosen by 5-fold cross-fitting with fold assignment fixed once (seed 7, stratified by label). For each fold, tau* = the tau with the largest coverage meeting the A1 conditions on the other 4 folds; if no tau is feasible on those 4 folds, tau* = infinity (that fold contributes coverage 0). tau* is applied to the held-out fold. A1 coverage and selective kappa are computed on the pooled held-out accepts. The single deployable threshold (tau fitted on all n) is reported separately; the in-sample sweep is only an upper-bound sensitivity.
- Secondary (A2, success side): cascade kappa on all n items (pooled out-of-fold) when rejected items take the LM's prediction. PRIMARY LM = Qwen3-4B (best job-03 RAGTruth kappa); Phi-4-mini is exploratory. Success needs A2 >= target. Also report LM calls saved = coverage, and cost = (1 - coverage) x LM p50 latency.
- 95% CI (nested): 1000 bootstrap resamples (seed 7) of items with replacement, expressed as integer weights per original item. In EACH resample the whole pipeline is re-run: fresh stratified 5-fold assignment of the original items, threshold fit on the training folds using the weights, application to the held-out folds, pooling. Weighting (instead of duplicating rows) keeps duplicates of one item in the same fold, so no train/test leakage. Percentile 95% intervals for A1 coverage, selective kappa, A2 and (cascade error - Qwen-alone error). The point estimate uses the fixed fold assignment (seed 7) with unit weights.
Decision rules (owner-approved 2026-10-28):
- KILL A if out-of-fold A1 coverage < 20% on both RAGTruth and WiCE (point estimate).
- SCALE A if out-of-fold A1 coverage >= 20% on both sets AND A2 (Qwen3-4B) >= target on both sets, with the bootstrap lower bound of A1 coverage > 0 on both. Single primary LM, so no multiplicity across LMs. Still reported as "to be confirmed on fresh items".
- Otherwise ITERATE.
Exploratory (not decision-bearing): Phi-4-mini cascade; calib-tuned cut instead of 0.5; confidence from the LMs instead of MiniCheck; a stacked (MiniCheck x LM) confidence; the other three sets (XSum, CNN, ExpertQA); A on the natural-mix 900 items of B, including applying the A threshold out-of-domain there (no Sonnet benchmark, so only coverage, selective risk, risk-coverage and AURC). Limitations stated: (1) the A sets are class-balanced by construction (RAIM-matched), which makes the Sonnet constant comparable but is not the natural mix, and the 300 RAGTruth items are generator-skewed (148 of 300 are gpt-4); (2) Qwen3-4B was picked as primary after seeing job-03 results on these same sets, so A is a re-analysis of existing data, not an independent confirmation.

## B. Rank systems, not items
Hypothesis B (a pilot with 6 units: at n=6 a two-sided permutation test needs tau >= 0.73 for p < 0.05, so tau-b = 0.5 is not statistically significant by itself; the rule below gates on tier order, not on significance): a small judge, applied to a generator's outputs, orders the six RAGTruth generators the way the human labels do.
Method: per generator g and judge j, judged unfaithful rate U(j,g) = share of the generator's 150 items with pred = 0 at the fixed 0.5 cut. Human rate H(g) as above. Ranking quality = Kendall's tau-b between H and U(j,.) over the 6 generators (15 pairs). With P concordant and Q discordant pairs, n0 = n(n-1)/2, n1 = sum over tied groups in H of t(t-1)/2, n2 = same for U: tau-b = (P - Q) / sqrt((n0 - n1)(n0 - n2)) (Kendall 1938 defines tau = (P - Q)/n0, tau-a; the tie-corrected tau-b is Kendall 1945).
CI: stratified bootstrap over items within each generator (1000 resamples, seed 7), recomputing BOTH H and U on every resample (the human rates are themselves estimated from 150 items per generator, so they are resampled, not fixed). Resamples where tau-b is undefined (all ties) are counted tau-b = 0 (conservative toward KILL) in the primary CI; the count is reported, and the CI with them dropped is a sensitivity. An exact permutation p-value of the observed tau-b over the 720 orderings is also reported (descriptive).
Primary judge for B = Qwen3-4B (best job-03 RAGTruth kappa); MiniCheck and Phi-4-mini are exploratory. Tiers: High = llama-2-7b; Mid = llama-2-13b, llama-2-70b, mistral-7B; Low = gpt-3.5, gpt-4. Tier order is correct iff, over individual generators, U(High) > max U(Mid) and min U(Mid) > max U(Low).
Decision rules (owner-approved 2026-10-28): KILL B if the primary judge has a tier inversion (tier order not correct, defined above) or tau-b < 0.3. SCALE B if tau-b >= 0.5 and tier order is correct (the bootstrap CI is reported but not gated). Else ITERATE, defined as: one further round only, limited to changes to the prompt, the cut or the judge model, re-registered as a new prereg; no third round without owner OK.
Noted alternative, NOT used (owner's first proposal): KILL B if tau-b < 0.5 or the 95% CI includes 0. Dropped because with n=6 a CI excludes 0 essentially only for tau-b >= 0.87, which makes ITERATE unreachable.
Power caveat stated up front: with n=6 the tau values are multiples of 2/15 and the CI is wide; differences of 1 to 6 items among the Mid generators are noise. A pass means the three tiers are recovered, not the fine order inside Mid. Tier-level concordance (the tier-order check above) is the more robust of the two criteria.
Secondary (exploratory): MiniCheck and Phi-4-mini tau-b; item-level Cohen's kappa and bacc of each judge on the 900 (natural, imbalanced mix; kappa not comparable to the balanced job-03 numbers); mean(1 - p_yes) as an alternative generator score; Spearman rho as a cross-check. The train split is NOT run (owner decision 2026-10-28). No other metrics are defined.

## Status of the evidence
A and B are re-analyses and pilots on public data with models already seen in job 03; they are not independent confirmatory tests. Any SCALE call means "worth a confirmatory run on fresh data", nothing more.
